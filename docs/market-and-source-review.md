# Market and source review

Review date: 25 October 2026. The original report is preserved unchanged in the
private project archive. The following findings amend the build assumptions.

## The opportunity must be demonstrated

The report's prevention-first thesis is a useful product hypothesis. Claims
that most disputes will disappear, that insurers or marketplaces will pay,
or that a precedent registry will become defensible are untested. This release
contains no independent user result, paid demand or real dispute outcome.

A generic typed-contract compiler or MCP interface is insufficient differentiation.
The first product experiment is a clear, narrow acceptance profile with useful
counterexamples and a reproducible dossier that another reviewer can inspect.
Its value must be compared with the customer's existing process.

## Current primary-source findings

1. **Accord Project overlaps the contract layer.** Its official documentation
   now expressly describes agreements, obligations and remedies for agentic
   commerce; its AI workflow documentation includes MCP contract tools.
   Evaluate compatibility before inventing a wider contract language. This
   documentation establishes capability claims, not a partnership with Piepowder.
   [Agentic commerce](https://docs.accordproject.org/docs/accordproject-agentic-commerce/)
   and [AI workflows](https://docs.accordproject.org/docs/accordproject-ai/).

2. **ADRP has advanced beyond the report's version.** The current record is
   `draft-stone-adrp-01`, updated 4 September 2026 and expiring 8 March 2027.
   It includes acceptance criteria and principal-linked arbitration mandates.
   Its missed-deadline treatment includes a hold-and-reassign recommendation,
   alongside older inconsistent refund language. Any adapter must resolve that
   versioned interpretation; it remains an individual Internet-Draft.
   [IETF record and text](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-stone-adrp/).

3. **ADP is an archived individual draft.** The current record for Kotecha's
   `-00` submission is expired and archived. It is neither an endorsed standard
   nor evidence of live adoption.
   [IETF status record](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kotecha-agentic-dispute-protocol/).

4. **ERC-8183's evaluator is an address.** Its minimal protocol has no appeal
   process, but that address may be a smart contract aggregating other signals.
   The current draft's terminal and expiry semantics matter to any future
   integration. Piepowder cannot promise review after an irreversible terminal
   action has already occurred.
   [Current ERC-8183 draft](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8183).

5. **Institutional AI arbitration already exists.** AAA-ICDR's 3 November 2025
   announcement supports the report's description of voluntary documents-only,
   two-party construction cases, with party validation and human authorisation.
   Piepowder has no relationship with the institution and does not offer its service.
   [AAA announcement](https://www.adr.org/press-releases/aaa-icdr-ai-arbitrator-now-available/).

6. **Kleros supplies stronger first-party evidence than the report's republication.**
   Its 9 October 2026 development update describes ClawBank dispute activity and
   an AI-to-human appeal gate under development. This supports pilot activity,
   without establishing scale, commercial volume or enforceability.
   [Kleros development update](https://blog.kleros.io/kleros-development-update-september-2026/).

The audit found stale versions and incomplete comparisons. It does not support
calling Piepowder the first agent court. Research and vendor demonstrations also
do not establish customer willingness to pay. The source review should be refreshed
when a concrete implementation or market decision depends on a changed specification.
