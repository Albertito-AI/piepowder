# Mathematical qualifications

The founding report's arithmetic supplies useful illustrations. Its numerical
inputs do not describe measured Piepowder customers, judges or operating results.

## Accuracy and ambiguity

The worked sample gives 972/1000 = 97.2% when all sampled records count, and
972/983 = 98.8809766…% when flagged records are excluded. The engine compares
integer products, so display rounding cannot make a failing threshold pass.
Which denominator the parties agreed is a separate question. Counting records
does not establish representative sampling or the reference service's accuracy.

## Majority panels

For independent binary assessors, each correct with probability 0.8, majority
accuracy is 0.896 for three, 0.94208 for five and 0.966656 for seven assessors.
These are conditional binomial calculations. Independence and a shared, known
per-assessor accuracy have not been established for a Piepowder panel.

The expression `n / (1 + (n - 1) * rho)` is a variance-equivalent effective
sample-size heuristic for an exchangeable average. It is not a formula for
majority correctness. Marginal accuracy and pairwise correlation do not fix
the joint distribution of votes. Different model vendors do not guarantee
independent errors, and a 4–1 split does not establish a 7% chance of reversal.

## Disagreement scores

For readings distributed 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1, entropy is approximately 1.295462 bits,
normalised entropy 0.817345, and the minority disagreement rate is 0.4. These
describe recorded answers. Shared mistakes can produce agreement, so none is
a calibrated probability of legally ambiguous meaning or a tribunal's decision.
This release has no multi-model linter and publishes no semantic precision or recall.

## Settlement and appeals

In the report's risk-neutral illustration, a settlement interval exists when
the claimant's minimum `pC*J - cC` does not exceed the respondent's maximum
`pR*J + cR`. Lower adjudication cost can narrow the interval if beliefs do not
also converge. That is a model result, not an empirical claim about this market.

The refundable-fee appeal threshold `F / (S + F)` is 9.0909…% for a fee of 200
and a stake of 2000 under the report's simplified assumptions. An operational
decision also depends on successful correction, further costs, delay, remedies
and risk preferences. A falling reversal rate alone does not justify increasing
fees; a roughly even win rate alone does not validate the precedent registry.

Future simulations must report assumptions, sensitivity, selection effects and
failed scenarios. No fee-setting or legal decision is automated by these examples.
